02

Gurukul Stories

AI Storytelling & Curriculum Design
ClientGurukul, a Seattle organisation teaching Indian-origin children their heritage language and culture
AudienceChildren from grade one up, English at school, Hindi at home
Where & whenSeattle · 2026
First title complete · series in production

Hindi classes for kids in Seattle run on books written for kids in India. The words assume a street these children have never walked down. A child listens to the story they recognise, and the story was somewhere else.

So we are making the books. Illustrated readers in Hindi, graded from first grade up, written for a child who speaks English all day and hears Hindi at home. The art is generated and directed rather than commissioned, which is the only reason a community organisation gets a library instead of four titles.

Generating images is the easy half. Holding one world across a whole series, in a script most models render badly, is the work. Every spread gets the same review as paying client work.

  • First title done: Ek Laal Gend Ka Rahasya, forty pages, grade one
  • Characters, palette and setting held across every spread
03

diseasefree.living

Brand Identity, Website, Audience Strategy & Product Architecture
ClientDr. Virender Sodhi, naturopathic physician, 46 years in practice, 30,000+ patients treated and counting
AudienceAdults managing chronic conditions, US and India
Where & whenSeattle · 2025 to now
Audience live · platform design complete

Forty-six years of clinical authority, almost none of it leaving the consult room. Behind that sat the harder problem: he wanted to build a preventive-health platform and had no audience to launch it to.

So I built the audience first. Podcasts, short video, editorial, webinars and a paid newsletter, each format feeding the next, AI running the production line so two people make what a content team makes. Reach was never the point. A warm market on launch day was.

Then the brand it would all sit under. I designed the identity and built diseasefree.living: the mark, the typography, the voice, and every page of the site, written around three kinds of patient rather than one generic visitor.

Then the platform. Models improvise and medicine cannot, so it generates nothing: every answer is a protocol he approved, picked by a rules engine, checked on his dashboard before anyone sees it. It runs on WhatsApp, because nobody should download an app to be cared for. I designed it end to end and ran the first live test myself.

  • Paid newsletter, five months, zero unsubscribes
  • Launch webinar: 101 registered, 53% live attendance, 100+ early adopters

YOU Summit

Founding Creative & Design Partner
ClientYOU Summit, a 1,500-attendee AI conference
AudienceStudents and early-career people entering an AI job market
Where & whenUniversity of Washington · 8–10 January 2027
Organic channel live · campaign rebuilding for January

The summit exists to make young people confident with AI. Hiring an agency to market it would have contradicted the whole premise, so a student team runs it, trained and directed by me.

The identity is built to be run by people who did not design it, with a template library precise enough that a first-timer still makes something good. I also designed the AI Film Sprint that closes the final day.

We piloted nine channels. Reddit compounded, and now reaches 333,000 views a post and climbing. When the summit moved to January I kept that one running and paused the rest. No sense spending against a date that no longer existed.

05

Feedo

Brand, Product Design & Strategy
ClientCahn Studios, built in-house as a product bet
AudienceArt editors and video teams reviewing cuts with clients
Where & whenSeattle · 2025, six months live
Built · validated · capital redirected

A fully functional video-feedback platform with paid subscriptions. Brand and interface mine, prototyped in Lovable before a developer took it to production. Three months from idea to a product people paid for, then six months live: 50+ onboardings in the first two, and 300 conversations with video editors and buyers behind it. On ease of use it does what Frame.io does now, and we had it first.

Then the arithmetic. The next phase needed a far deeper AI layer, and with no engineers in-house that meant serious investment. I sized the market against three years of carrying that cost and could not get it past a million dollars without being acquired. Adobe had just bought Frame.io. So we stopped building and kept the work, and all of it carried into the health platform.

How I Run a Team

Direction, then room

I set the standard and the direction, then hand over execution. The YOU Summit campaign is made by a student team I trained. I review it. I do not rewrite it, because rewriting someone's work teaches them nothing.

YOU Summit · nine channels · student team

A review gate, not a bottleneck

Everything passes me inside twenty-four hours. Playbooks, brand guidelines and a versioned template library mean most of it passes first time, which is the point. The standard holds when I am not in the room.

24-hour turnaround · nine platforms · one standard

The date is part of the brief

A deadline is a creative constraint, not an administrative one. Across fifteen years and three studios the work has gone out when it was promised, and teams I have led have not needed heroics to get there.

80%+ client retention across 50+ brands

About

Creative executive and AI strategist in Seattle. 15+ years across brand, product, and film, and three ventures founded and run.

My education runs in two directions: a B.Tech (Hons) in Computer Science Engineering, then a Masters in Animation from the Academy of Art University in San Francisco. The engineering degree is not why I would build a system. It is why I can specify one, read what an engineering team tells me, and argue the trade-offs without a translator. The second degree is where the standard comes from. It is also why my strategy tends to survive execution: I have usually made the thing I am arguing for.

At Cahn Studios I architect AI products, direct brands, and build the teams that deliver them. Before Seattle I co-founded Arteq Studios in Gurgaon and led a team of eight through five hundred pieces of work for Nestlé, Lenskart, Urban Company, the Embassy of Switzerland, and Rupa Publications.

I still draw, and I teach drawing at Seattle Art Circle. The next decade's creative organizations will be AI-native. I would rather build one than watch it happen.
